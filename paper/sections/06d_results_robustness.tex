\subsection{Robustness and generalisation}\label{sec:robust}
We evaluated all policies \emph{zero-shot}, without retraining or re-tuning, under shifted conditions: different levels of sensor noise at test time, resilient and fatigue-prone worker profiles (all four rate parameters scaled by $\pm30\%$), and lighter or heavier workloads (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}; full results in Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:s-robust}). We also trained five seeds of a domain-randomised agent (AA-DQN-DR) on randomly sampled workloads ($N\in\{8,\dots,16\}$), worker profiles ($\pm30\%$ on all rate parameters) and noise levels ($\sigma_o\in[0.05,0.2]$).

\emph{Sensor noise.} The learned policies are insensitive to the test-time noise level over the range they were trained near. The returns of both AA-DQN variants vary by less than 0.15 between $\sigma_o=0$ and $\sigma_o=0.3$, and those of AA-PPO by less than 0.05 up to $\sigma_o=0.2$; at $\sigma_o=0.3$, three times the training level, AA-PPO loses 0.3--0.5 but remains the best method. The threshold rules, in contrast, degrade as noise grows, because spurious threshold crossings trigger unnecessary breaks. SPT-Threshold falls from 12.30 to 11.35 and from 71.4\% to 65.6\% on-time, and EDF-Threshold falls from 11.35 to 9.63. The periodic rules are unaffected, because they ignore the cognitive signals altogether. The history-based policies average out noise that a threshold applied to a single reading cannot.

\emph{Worker profiles.} \rev{AA-PPO is the best method for both profiles: for fatigue-prone workers its return is 11.04 (SPT-Threshold 10.59, AA-DQN 10.05), and for resilient workers, who need less rest, the intensity-only AA-PPO reaches 15.46, ahead of the EDF-based rules (EDF-Periodic 15.01), which are the best rules for this profile. AA-DQN, in contrast, is overtaken by SPT-Threshold for fatigue-prone workers and by the EDF-based rules for resilient workers. The best rule depends on the profile, SPT-based for fatigue-prone and EDF-based for resilient workers, whereas the same AA-PPO policy is best for both.}\revnote{R4.2}

\emph{Workload.} \rev{Generalisation to a different number of tasks per day is the clearest weakness of AA-DQN and is much smaller for AA-PPO. With 16 tasks per day, both AA-PPO variants remain the best methods (11.83 versus 11.61 for SPT-Threshold), whereas AA-DQN falls well behind the SPT-based rules (9.69--10.03). With 8 tasks, the EDF-based rules complete almost all tasks on time and attain the highest return (EDF-Threshold 13.12); the intensity-only AA-PPO is close behind (13.04, with the same on-time rate of 97.4\%), and AA-DQN falls behind (11.71--12.20).} Although the observation is normalised by $N$, the queue statistics of these days lie outside the training distribution. Domain randomisation of AA-DQN improved the 16-task result (10.35) but did not close the gap and lowered nominal performance (11.63), so a simple randomisation over conditions with checkpoint selection on nominal days is insufficient. \rev{The better generalisation of AA-PPO suggests that the stochastic, on-policy learner finds smoother policies that depend less on the exact queue statistics, but we did not investigate the mechanism.}
