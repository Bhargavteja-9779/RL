\subsection{What do the agents learn?}\label{sec:behaviour}
Figure~\ref{fig:behaviour} shows how actions and latent states evolve through the day, aggregated over the 500 test days (and over the ten seeds for the learned policies). Three behavioural regularities distinguish the learned policies from the rules.

\emph{Work while fresh.} Both learners almost never rest in the first hour (AA-PPO 1.9\% and AA-DQN 0.5\% of slots, versus 3.6\% for SPT-Threshold, 4.6\% for EDF-Threshold and a fixed 16.7\% for EDF-Periodic), and both devote about half of the first hour to hard tasks (AA-PPO 54\%, AA-DQN 50\%), when attention is highest. SPT-Threshold does almost no hard work in the first hour (8\%), because hard tasks are long. A fixed cycle cannot distinguish a fresh worker at 09:30 from a recovered one at 15:00, and a threshold rule reacts only once a threshold has been crossed.

\emph{Rest when it is cheapest, and protect the middle of the day.} AA-DQN's break share peaks at 24.6\% between 13:00 and 14:00, which coincides with the modelled post-lunch dip, and declines afterwards; in the 13:00--15:00 window it rests in 22.4\% of slots, versus 12.5\% at other times. Resting is cheapest when the productivity it forgoes is lowest (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho}). \rev{AA-PPO rests earlier and more evenly: 17\% of slots between 10:00 and 11:00 and 24--27\% between 11:00 and 14:00, before and during the dip, but only 9--11\% after 15:00. As a result, its mean fatigue stays between 0.27 and 0.30 from 10:30 to 14:30, and mean attention never falls below 0.73. In the last two hours, AA-PPO works almost continuously to clear the remaining deadlines and lets fatigue rise to 0.45 at the end of the day. This is rational in a single-day model, in which end-of-day fatigue carries no future cost, but it would be penalised in a multi-day setting (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).}\revnote{R4.4} The threshold rules show a single, sharper peak (35--36\% between 13:00 and 14:00) only because the dip pushes observed attention below their fixed threshold.

\emph{Match difficulty to capacity without starving hard tasks.} The learned policies devote the most hard work to the morning and the least to the afternoon trough: AA-DQN goes from 50\% at 09:00--11:00 to 33\% at 13:00--15:00, and AA-PPO from 54\% in the first hour to 18\% between 13:00 and 14:00, filling the trough with easy tasks. Both return to hard work in the last hour (AA-DQN 48\%, AA-PPO 78\%) as the deadlines of the remaining hard tasks approach. SPT-Threshold postpones hard tasks until they become urgent and spends 82\% of the last hour on hard work, when hard tasks are slowest (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho}), without having done hard work in the morning. This back-loading explains its low difficulty-weighted on-time rate (Section~\ref{sec:main}). EDF, in contrast, does more hard work in the afternoon (71\%) than in the morning (60\%) and never rests. The hand-crafted Cog-Heuristic was designed to capture the difficulty--attention intuition but, with a single attention threshold, also pushes hard tasks into the late afternoon (67\% after 16:00). The learned policies combine early hard work, easy work in the trough and late deadline-driven hard work with short-task completion, which illustrates how hard it is to encode such time-, state- and deadline-dependent trade-offs in rules.

Compared with the memoryless DQN, AA-DQN rests more (\AADQNBreakPct\% versus \DQNBreakPct\% of slots) and takes breaks at a lower true fatigue level (0.36 versus 0.44); AA-PPO rests at a still lower level (0.31). The DQN concentrates its rest in the last hour (29\% of slots after 16:00), consistent with late, reactive breaks triggered by single noisy readings.
