\begin{abstract}
Classical dispatching rules such as earliest-deadline-first (EDF) and shortest-processing-time (SPT) assume that the resource executing work has constant capacity. This assumption fails when the resource is a person whose attention is depleted by sustained effort, whose fatigue accumulates, and whose alertness follows a circadian rhythm. This paper formulates human-centric task scheduling as a partially observable Markov decision process. Rest is an explicit action, attention and fatigue are latent states observed only through noisy estimates, and a multi-objective reward balances deadline-weighted task value against cognitive load. We propose an attention-aware deep Q-network (AA-DQN) that combines a short observation history, which approximates the belief state, with Double Q-learning, a dueling architecture and a hybrid action space of workload-intensity choices and dispatching rules. In a cognitively grounded simulator with stochastic task arrivals, AA-DQN was benchmarked against nine rule-based schedulers, including grid-tuned EDF and SPT variants that rest periodically or when observed fatigue or attention crosses a threshold, using ten training seeds, 500 held-out workdays with common random numbers, and multiplicity-corrected paired tests. Rules that never rest performed poorly (EDF: \edfOnTimePct\% of tasks on time). AA-DQN matched the return of the best tuned rule (difference $\dRetSPTThreshold$, 95\% CI [\dRetSPTThresholdLo, \dRetSPTThresholdHi]) and achieved the highest on-time rate (\aaOnTimePct\% versus \bestHeurOnTimePct\%). Unlike any single rule, it combined this with high difficulty-weighted completion, avoiding the neglect of demanding tasks that afflicts SPT-based rules. The learned policy works intensively while the worker is fresh, concentrates breaks around the post-lunch circadian dip, and schedules demanding tasks when attention is high. Ablations and zero-shot tests identify when learning adds most value, namely with reliable cognitive-state estimates and with fatigue-prone workers, and where it remains limited.
\end{abstract}
